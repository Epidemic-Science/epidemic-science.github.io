A new preprint led by Paulo C. Ventura looks at how the average length and the shape of the
latent and infectious periods change how an epidemic unfolds, using simulations of transmission
in households, schools and workplaces.

Comparing parameters drawn from viral-load data with traditional assumptions, the study finds
that shorter periods go with higher reproduction numbers, and that the timing and size of the
epidemic peak differ markedly between the two. Authors include members of the CEPH Lab and SEALab.
